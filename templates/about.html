RaceOpt / Field notes

From data to decisions

RaceOpt processes F1 race strategy from real lap telemetry end to end — from pulling raw data, training a lap-time prediction model and simulating the full grid, to searching for faster alternative strategies.

Scope of this tool

RaceOpt is an independent analysis platform built for educational purposes. It is not an official product and is not associated with, endorsed by, or affiliated with Formula 1, the FIA, any racing team, or related trademarks. All figures come from a statistical model trained on past data, not a forecast of future race results, and it does not model factors such as car development, real-time rival team strategy, or in-race driver decisions.

Data — FastF1 lap telemetry

Where every number comes from

Every number on this site starts with FastF1 — a library that pulls official F1 timing/telemetry and turns it into a per-driver, lap-by-lap dataset: lap times, sector times, tyre compound, tyre age, track status, weather and the real starting grid. Data is cached on disk after the first fetch (see the cache/ folder) so pages don't re-request it on every load.

02

Model — Random Forest Regressor

Predicting lap time

A RandomForestRegressor trained on real lap telemetry predicts "lap time" from features such as tyre age, tyre compound, estimated fuel remaining and the current stint — validated by comparing predicted against real lap times for drivers not in the training set. Full hyperparameter and feature details are in the Model Report

03

Simulation — the whole grid

Strategy Lab

Once a strategy is set (tyres + pit laps), the system builds features for every lap of that strategy, has the model predict each lap time, adds a pace offset calibrated from the real driver plus the pit loss estimated for that circuit, and sums it into a total race time — done for the circuit's entire real grid at once to rank the field and compute win probability.

04

Optimization — finding faster strategies

Strategy Analysis

A grid search runs through every feasible tyre/pit-lap combination for that circuit, compares total race time with the driver's real strategy, then classifies the faster strategies as undercut/overcut by pit lap relative to the baseline. Details and a glossary are in Strategy Analysis
